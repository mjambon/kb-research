I found three potential anchor relocation issues:

- **P0 — Relocation depends on indexing order.** After an attempt fails, [the anchor records the attempted revision](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:491). [Later updates only retry anchors attached to the document being updated](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:525). If text moves from `a.ml` to `b.ml` and `a.ml` is indexed first, the anchor becomes outdated; indexing `b.ml` afterward cannot recover it. This can happen within one source update, which [indexes files sequentially](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/cli/Documents_update_cmd.ml:438).

- **P0 — A match can silently move an anchor to unrelated text.** [The highest-scoring candidate is applied without checking document relationship](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:483). A test [demonstrates an anchor moving to an unrelated file that shares a verbatim block](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/base-libs/text/tests/Text_api_tests.ml:1097). The stored document and contents then change, so links can point to the wrong evidence while appearing up to date.

- **P1 — Repeated text can prevent relocation.** [Indexing discards every occurrence of a shingle repeated within a document](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-stripped-comments-markdown/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:151). If a moved passage appears twice in its destination, it may yield no searchable hits and leave its anchor outdated even though the text is present.
