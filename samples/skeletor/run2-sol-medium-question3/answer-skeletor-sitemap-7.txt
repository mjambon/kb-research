I found three potential issues with anchor relocation:

- **P0 — Relocation depends on indexing order.** A failed attempt is recorded against the original document’s revision, and only anchors on a subsequently edited document are retried. If text moves to another file but the original is indexed first, adding the destination later leaves the anchor stale until the original changes again. See [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:481) and [DB.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/DB.ml:852).

- **P0 — An unrelated copy can silently take ownership of an anchor.** Candidates are ranked by text match, with no preference for the original document or evidence that the copy moved. The existing test demonstrates an anchor relocating to unrelated boilerplate after its original text is replaced; it then appears up to date. See [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:348) and [Text_api_tests.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/tests/Text_api_tests.ml:1097).

- **P1 — Repeated text cannot be relocated.** Shingle extraction discards *every* occurrence of a repeated shingle, including the first. An anchor made of repetitive text can therefore have no query hits even when an exact copy exists at its new location. This is covered as a current limitation in [Anchor_algorithm_tests.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/tests/Anchor_algorithm_tests.ml:102); the filtering is in [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-sitemap/src/skeletor/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:154).
