I found four potential issues in anchor relocation:

- **P0 — An intact anchor can move to a duplicate in another file.** Candidate ranking breaks equal text matches by document ID, with no preference for the anchor’s current document. An unrelated edit can therefore silently change what document the anchor cites. [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-cm+answer/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:355)

- **P0 — Relocation can cross source boundaries.** Hit collection searches the global shingle index without filtering by the anchor’s source. Deleting a snippet from one repository can attach its anchor to identical, unrelated text in another. [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-cm+answer/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:206)

- **P0 — A moved anchor can later become stale without being flagged for review.** Relocation retains `acknowledged_revision`, but review compares that number with the destination document’s revision. Moving from a high revision document to a low revision one can suppress review after a failed later relocation. [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-cm+answer/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:116) [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-cm+answer/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:512)

- **P1 — Repeated text can produce no relocation hits.** Shingle indexing discards every occurrence of a shingle that appears more than once in a document. A short anchor made of such text can become stale after an unrelated edit even while its text remains intact. [Anchor.ml](/workspace/kb-research/samples/skeletor/skeletor-cm+answer/base-libs/text/Anchor.ml:159)
